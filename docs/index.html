Who talks about housing density and transit at Bay Area public meetings

  • I collected a year of Bay Area council, county and transit board meetings, and transcribed the stretches about housing density or transit.
  • I had a large language model label each public speaker NIMBY, meaning against new housing or transit nearby, YIMBY, meaning for it, or neither, after a second pass checked which way each proposal pointed.
  • Corrected that way the public split is too close to call, and so is almost every body, though public speakers still took a side more often than the officials. The plots below carry the numbers and every limitation is on the Methods page.

These meetings decide real projects. San José Spotlight reported that in June 2025 the San Jose City Council voted unanimously to deny 135 homes, after neighbors objected.

Blocking housing density and transit keeps the next generation out of the places with the jobs, and keeps housing costs high. It also locks in car dependence: longer journeys, higher household travel costs, more traffic deaths and more tailpipe pollution.

One comment against more housing, one for it

NIMBY (against more housing)
“Mayor and council members, reject the missing middle framework recommendation in the general plan for your review. San Jose residents do not want three-story apartment complexes by right in residential neighborhoods without parking. Smart growth means concentrating density along commercial corridors, transit hubs, not quiet residential streets. …”

A member of the public, San José City Council, 2026-08-18.

YIMBY (for more of it)
“… I'm somebody who is very interested in housing in Fremont, and from what I can tell, this changing with the zoning does improve the ability for us to actually create more housing …”

A member of the public, Fremont City Council, 2026-05-05.
