thtrans: who talks about housing density and transit at Bay Area public meetings

  • I collected a year of city council, county board and transit board meetings from around the Bay Area. I transcribed the stretches where housing density or transit was the item on the agenda.
  • I had a large language model score each statement from its transcribed words. Each score says whether the speaker supported or opposed the item, and whether the speaker was hostile.
  • I found that public speakers came out for or against the item more often than the officials who ran the meeting. Their statements were also coded hostile more often.
  • The plots below carry the numbers. Every instrument, limitation and version is on the Methods page.

NIMBY and YIMBY name the two sides of this argument: Wikipedia defines NIMBY and YIMBY. These meetings decide real projects. San José Spotlight reported that in June 2025 the San Jose City Council voted unanimously to deny 135 homes, after neighbors objected.

Blocking housing density and transit keeps the next generation out of the places with the jobs, and keeps housing costs high. It also locks in car dependence: longer journeys, higher household travel costs, more traffic deaths and more tailpipe pollution.

One comment against the housing item, one for it

NIMBY (against the housing item)
“Mayor and council members, reject the missing middle framework recommendation in the general plan for your review. San Jose residents do not want three-story apartment complexes by right in residential neighborhoods without parking. Smart growth means concentrating density along commercial corridors, transit hubs, not quiet residential streets. …”

A member of the public, San José City Council, 2026-08-18. This speaker's statements average -2.0, on the scale from -2 to +2.

YIMBY (for it)
“… I'm somebody who is very interested in housing in Fremont, and from what I can tell, this changing with the zoning does improve the ability for us to actually create more housing …”

A member of the public, Fremont City Council, 2026-05-05. This speaker's statements average +1.0, on the same scale.
